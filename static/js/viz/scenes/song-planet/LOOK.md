# Song planet: the look

## Art direction (the target)

No target image was generated: this environment has no image generator, and no reference picture was supplied (see `design.md`, "Target images for dream-loop"). The target is this text, and the dream-loop judge scores screenshots against it.

- **Subject.** A small low-poly planet, alone in a dark starry space, seen whole with a margin, from a little above its equator. The song is wrapped once around it: going round the planet is going through the song.
- **Terrain.** Each stem is a ring of faceted terrain around the planet at its own latitude, vocals furthest north, in StemDeck's order, each in its stem's colour and no other. A ring rises where its stem is loud and sinks to the ground where it is quiet, so a ring's skyline is that stem's loudness through the song. Brighter along its crest and where it is louder. Rings are separated by a thin strip of bare ground.
- **Ground and sections.** The ground under and between the rings, and both polar caps, are dark blue-grey. Where the song has sections, each section's stretch of the planet, pole to pole, is tinted with that section's own colour, so sections read as differently coloured regions.
- **Notes.** Where a stem has notes, small pale crystals stand on its ring, one per note, placed across the ring by pitch and as tall as the note was played hard. They are texture, not the main shape.
- **The present moment.** A thin bright white meridian from pole to pole, always facing the viewer in the middle of the picture. The half of the planet to its right, the part of the song still to come, lies under a light shadow.
- **Light.** One sun that travels with the viewer, so the visible face is always lit, with flat-shaded facets catching it. A faint blue rim of air around the planet. Small still stars. No glow except a little on the meridian.
- **Mute.** A muted stem's ring keeps its shape and goes almost black.
- **Camera.** The viewpoint travels round the planet as the song plays, one lap for one song, always looking at its centre. The stars stay still, which is what shows the travel.
- **Calm version (reduce motion).** The viewpoint stays where it is and the planet turns slowly under it instead, one turn for one song. No glow.

## Songs it was checked on

Three songs from the developer's library, in a preview copy of it, on 2026-11-22. The task asked for "dense rock, sparse acoustic, and one with no notes and no sections"; the library had three songs, so these are what they were, described by their data:

- **Song A**, "change-my-mind": 207 s, every stem transcribed (5,365 notes), one marked section from 98 s to 123 s.
- **Song B**, "Don't You Worry 'Bout A Thing": 285 s, viewed with no notes and no sections, so there are no crystals and the ground is one colour.
- **Song C**, "Old Town Road": 157 s, every stem transcribed (2,204 notes), no sections.

## Critique

dream-loop's judge loop: an independent agent with a fresh context each round, given only the art direction above and screenshots (song A at 62 s, 150 s and, from round 2, 110 s inside its section; song B at 95 s; song C at 45 s; and song A at 62 s with the guitar muted), scoring with dream-loop's rubric: composition 0 to 3, lighting 0 to 3, materials 0 to 3, details 0 to 1. Pass is 8 of 10.

| 3 | 2.5 | 2.45 | 2.5 | 0.75 | **8.2** | **pass** |

**What the last round's screenshots showed.** A small faceted planet whole in the picture among sparse stars, with six rings of terrain in the stems' colours from red in the north to blue-grey in the south and clear dark strips of ground between them. Ring crests are ragged where a stem comes and goes and brighter where it is loud. A thin white meridian runs down the middle and the half to its right is darker. Pale crystals stand on the rings in songs A and C and there are none in song B. Inside song A's section the ground from the north cap down through every gap is tinted navy. The muted guitar ring is near black with its shape kept and its crystals dark.

**What changed between rounds.** After round 1: each ring's height made relative to that stem's own loudest moment on a steep curve, so quiet passages sink to the ground; far fewer, larger facets; a deeper shadow over the half to come; a lower viewpoint and rings drawn nearer the equator; the air made a rim instead of a filled disc; a muted ring taken to near black; the section drawn as a tint over the ground. After round 2: a still lower viewpoint and light from below for the southern rings; a lighter shadow; a weaker section tint; a fainter, narrower rim; crystals rooted in the terrain as drawn and left out where a ring has sunk; the shadow raised to cover the crystals; stronger "brighter where louder"; lighter ground and wider strips.

**The judge's remaining gaps at 8.2**, most important first: the air shell still reads as a dark glass globe well outside the planet; quiet southern rings merge with the ground; the shadow on the half to come is moderate rather than light; the south cap is hidden and the meridian runs past the poles; the crystals on the drums' and bass's rings form a dense pale fringe; the vertical margin is tight.

**Changed after the last verdict.** Nothing.
